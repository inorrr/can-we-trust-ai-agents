# Initial selection audit — assistant observations, not student approval

The first valid editorial selection accepted all 15 eligible items. It did not produce the three rejected/deferred decisions needed for the student's required review sample. This does not prove every item is credible or independently corroborated. The source list was deliberately focused, which reduces the number of naturally irrelevant candidates.

Before considering the series final, inspect these decisions closely:

1. **Simon Willison's Wikimedia commentary versus Wikimedia's own report.** The commentary may add interpretation, but repeating the incident account is not independent confirmation. Identify its added analysis before using it as a distinct contribution.
2. **Willow's October product digest.** The vendor's description establishes what it claims to offer, not that controls work under attack or in production. Consider deferring efficacy claims unless there is evaluative evidence.
3. **Salt's runtime-protection architecture post.** Separate a proposed architecture or product claim from measured protection. Do not treat its relationship to Salt's Manus disclosure as independent validation of Salt's product.
4. **CSIS policy episodes.** Determine which passages add original policy analysis relevant to developers/product managers, and which only repeat external incident reports. Avoid treating repetition as corroboration or using unrelated mathematics/geopolitics segments to support the series.
5. **Sinofsky's framing of AI failures as software bugs.** This is an expert interpretation. It does not independently establish that operational controls are sufficient for every type of agent failure.

## Proposed direction for student-led policy revision

After the student reviews the actual sample, consider a stronger distinction between selecting a source to examine its claim and accepting the claim as established. Require selection reasons to state the exact usable contribution and what the source cannot establish. Reject redundant recaps without a distinct contribution; defer control-effectiveness claims without methods or measurable results. Preserve original policy/output before applying a revision.

Do not manufacture three rejections to satisfy a quota. Broaden normal discovery to include plausible adjacent material, or revise the policy based on the student's evidence-based judgment, then inspect the real decisions. This document is a suggestion for that review, not a completed policy comparison or a claim of human verification.

## Automated validation failures that were caught

- An early selection response omitted two eligible items. The component rejected it. The output schema was changed to require a keyed decision slot for every item; identifiers are bound to the actual input rather than generated by the model.
- An early brief used only two distinct publishers for piece 01. The component rejected it despite there being three source records. Brief generation now receives explicit publisher diversity requirements and can make one bounded revision using validation feedback.
- YouTube returned caption rate-limit errors. Media downloads plus OpenAI transcription were used as the recovery route. Two transcription uploads also failed with connection errors; receipts retain uncertain costs, and completed transcript chunks are cached for resume.

These observations are engineering and assistant editorial notes. The student still needs to review the source passages, complete the required decision sample, revise the policy, record actual factual corrections, and approve final content.
